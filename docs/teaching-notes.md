# Teaching notes

## Scope

The new core sets provide mixed Grade 5–7 practice, with extension questions for stronger students. They are not a complete Ontario curriculum course or a grade-by-grade assessment. In particular, some later questions ask students to combine ideas beyond a single grade's expectations.

Ontario's elementary mathematics framework includes number, algebra, data, spatial sense and financial literacy, alongside social-emotional learning and mathematical processes. The app's Number, Algebra, Geometry and Data filters are navigation labels, not official strand names. Money Decisions covers financial-literacy contexts within the Number filter.

Sources used to set the scope:

- Ontario Ministry of Education, [Mathematics curriculum, Grades 1–8](https://www.ontario.ca/page/math-curriculum-grades-1-8).
- University of Waterloo CEMC, [Math Circles](https://cemc.uwaterloo.ca/workshops/student-workshops/math-circles), as a model of accessible enrichment through explanations and problem solving.

The new questions and solutions were written for this app. They are not official CEMC problems, and the app is not affiliated with or endorsed by CEMC.

## Core practice

| Set | Starting knowledge | Later questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fractions & Fair Shares | Equal parts, multiplication and division | Recovering a whole from a changing fractional amount |
| Decimals, Percents & Ratios | Place value, equivalent fractions | Reverse percentages, sharing in ratios, average speed |
| Number Detectives | Factors, multiples, divisibility | Exactly-one counting, digit constraints |
| Patterns & Unknowns | Operations and simple patterns | Comparing relationships, growing matchstick patterns |
| Angles & Shapes | Basic shapes and angle measurement | Polygon angle sums and linked angle relationships |
| Area, Volume & Scale | Perimeter, rectangles, unit conversions | Compound shapes, borders, area and volume scale factors |
| Coordinates & Transformations | Reading ordered pairs | Negative coordinates and ordered transformations |
| Data & Chance | Sorting data, simple fractions | Recovering a mean, without-replacement probability |
| Money Decisions | Decimal arithmetic and percent | Unit prices, budgets, break-even costs, successive changes |

The tax rates and discounts in Money Decisions are given explicitly as assumptions within each problem. They are not presented as current tax or investment guidance.

## Enrichment

| Set | New idea | Starting knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clock Arithmetic | Remainders, cycles, final-digit patterns | Division with remainders |
| Binary & Secret Messages | Base two, shift ciphers, parity checks | Powers of two and the alphabet |
| Routes & Networks | Vertex degree, trees, Euler walks, shortest routes | Counting and small sums |
| Counting Without Listing | Product rule, combinations, encoded grid routes | Multiplication and systematic listing |
| Parity & Things That Stay Fixed | Invariants and impossibility arguments | Odd and even numbers |
| Socks, Drawers & Guarantees | Pigeonhole principle and worst-case reasoning | Grouping objects |
| Winning Moves | Backwards reasoning, losing positions, mirror strategies | Counting counters and subtraction |

Start these sets in order and untimed. Open the introduction before the round, let students try a small example, then use hints only after they have had time to explore. Solutions give the reasoning, including why an answer is guaranteed or an action is impossible. A race is optional; discussion is often better for a first encounter with an enrichment idea.

Difficulty dots describe position within each set, not a calibrated contest difficulty. Questions 1–4 establish the idea, 5–8 combine it with another step, and 9–12 ask for more reasoning. Students can revisit a completed round without the clock running.

## Diagrams

Every diagram has its givens written either in the prompt or the drawing, plus alternative text. Dimensions and angle constructions were computed in the generator. Read marked lengths and angles rather than estimating from a screen. Network crossings without a dot are not vertices. Geometry sets include both diagram questions and text-only questions.
