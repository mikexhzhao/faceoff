@fractions|Fractions & Fair Shares|Number|Core|Grades 5–7|½
!A fraction compares a number of equal parts with the number of parts in one whole. To compare fractions, give them the same denominator or compare each with one half.
!To add or subtract fractions, first express them in equal-sized parts. For example, $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$.
!To find a fraction of a quantity, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. When a fraction of the whole is known, work backwards to find the whole.
What fraction of the strip is shaded? || $\frac{3}{8}$ || Count all the equal sections, then the shaded sections. || There are 8 equal sections and 3 are shaded, so the shaded fraction is $\frac{3}{8}$. || fraction-strip || A strip divided into eight equal rectangles; the first three are shaded.
Write $\frac{6}{9}$ in lowest terms. || $\frac{2}{3}$ || Find a number that divides both 6 and 9. || Divide both the numerator and denominator by 3: $\frac{6}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$.
Which is greater: $\frac{5}{8}$ or $\frac{7}{12}$? || $\frac{5}{8}$ || Use a denominator of 24. || $\frac{5}{8}=\frac{15}{24}$ and $\frac{7}{12}=\frac{14}{24}$. Therefore $\frac{5}{8}$ is greater.
What is $\frac{3}{4}$ of 28? || $21$ || Find one quarter first. || One quarter is $28\div4=7$. Three quarters is $3\times7=21$.
Calculate $\frac{2}{3}+\frac{5}{6}$. || $\frac{3}{2}$ || Express both fractions in sixths. || $\frac{4}{6}+\frac{5}{6}=\frac{9}{6}=\frac{3}{2}$, or $1\frac{1}{2}$.
Calculate $\frac{7}{8}-\frac{1}{3}$. || $\frac{13}{24}$ || Both denominators divide 24. || $\frac{7}{8}-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{21}{24}-\frac{8}{24}=\frac{13}{24}$.
A jug holds $2\frac{1}{4}$ L of juice. How many full $\frac{3}{8}$ L cups can it fill? || $6$ || Write the amount in the jug in eighths. || The jug holds $\frac{18}{8}$ L. Each cup uses $\frac{3}{8}$ L, so it fills $18\div3=6$ cups.
Eighteen students are $\frac{3}{5}$ of a class. How many students are in the class? || $30$ || First find the number in one fifth. || One fifth is $18\div3=6$ students. Five fifths is $5\times6=30$ students.
A gardener plants $\frac{1}{3}$ of a plot with beans and $\frac{1}{4}$ of the same plot with carrots. The two regions do not overlap. What fraction remains? || $\frac{5}{12}$ || Subtract both planted fractions from one whole. || $1-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{12}{12}-\frac{4}{12}-\frac{3}{12}=\frac{5}{12}$.
A hiker walks $\frac{2}{3}$ of a trail before lunch, then half of the remaining distance. What fraction of the entire trail is still left? || $\frac{1}{6}$ || After lunch, the hiker completes half of the last third. || After the morning, $\frac{1}{3}$ remains. Half of that third is left at the end: $\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{6}$.
Which fraction with denominator 14 lies strictly between $\frac{3}{7}$ and $\frac{4}{7}$? || $\frac{7}{14}$ || Express the two endpoints in fourteenths. || The endpoints are $\frac{6}{14}$ and $\frac{8}{14}$. The only integer numerator strictly between 6 and 8 is 7.
A tank is $\frac{3}{4}$ full. After 18 L is removed, it is $\frac{3}{10}$ full. What is its capacity? || $40\text{ L}$ || The removed water represents the difference between the two fractions. || The removed fraction is $\frac{3}{4}-\frac{3}{10}=\frac{9}{20}$. One twentieth holds $18\div9=2$ L, so the whole tank holds $20\times2=40$ L.

!Percent means out of 100. For example, $35\%=\frac{35}{100}=0.35$. Ten percent and one percent are useful starting points for mental calculations.
!A ratio of $2:3$ divides a total into 5 equal-sized parts. Find one part before finding either share.
!A rate compares quantities with different units. Keep track of those units. When finding an average speed, divide total distance by total time.
Write $0.375$ as a fraction in lowest terms. || $\frac{3}{8}$ || Start with 375 thousandths. || $0.375=\frac{375}{1000}$. Divide numerator and denominator by 125 to obtain $\frac{3}{8}$.
Calculate $0.6+0.07+0.008$. || $0.678$ || Line up the decimal points. || The sum has 6 tenths, 7 hundredths and 8 thousandths: $0.678$.

What is $25\%$ of 80? || $20$ || Twenty-five percent is one quarter. || $80\div4=20$.
Eighteen out of 24 students chose a puzzle. What percent of the students chose it? || $75\%$ || Reduce $\frac{18}{24}$ first. || $\frac{18}{24}=\frac{3}{4}=\frac{75}{100}=75\%$.
Red and gold beads are in the ratio $3:5$. There are 40 beads altogether. How many are red? || $15$ || The 40 beads form 8 equal parts. || One part is $40\div8=5$ beads. Red beads occupy 3 parts, so there are $3\times5=15$.
A cyclist travels $1.8$ km in 12 minutes at a constant speed. How far will the cyclist travel in 30 minutes? || $4.5\text{ km}$ || Find the distance travelled in one minute. || The rate is $1.8\div12=0.15$ km per minute. In 30 minutes the distance is $0.15\times30=4.5$ km.

A drink uses concentrate and water in the ratio $2:7$. How many millilitres of concentrate are needed to make $1.8$ L of the drink? || $400\text{ mL}$ || Convert the total to millilitres and divide it into 9 parts. || $1.8$ L is 1800 mL. Each part is $1800\div9=200$ mL. The concentrate occupies 2 parts, or 400 mL.
A club has 25 members, and $40\%$ are girls. Five girls join and nobody leaves. What percent of the club members are now girls? || $50\%$ || Both the number of girls and the total membership change. || Initially there are $0.4\times25=10$ girls. Afterwards there are 15 girls out of 30 members, so the percentage is 50.
A hiker travels 3 km uphill at 4 km/h and 3 km downhill at 6 km/h, with no stops. What is the average speed for the whole trip? || $4.8\text{ km/h}$ || Add the two travel times before dividing the total distance by the total time. || The uphill time is $\frac{3}{4}$ h and the downhill time is $\frac{1}{2}$ h. The total is $\frac{5}{4}$ h for 6 km, giving $6\div\frac{5}{4}=4.8$ km/h.

!A factor divides a whole number with no remainder. A prime number has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.
!Common multiples help with repeating events. Common factors help when dividing different collections into identical groups without leftovers.
!A number is divisible by 3 or 9 when its digit sum is divisible by 3 or 9. It is divisible by 4 when the number formed by its last two digits is divisible by 4.
List all the prime numbers strictly between 10 and 20. || $11,13,17,19$ || Exclude even numbers and multiples of 3 or 5. || The four remaining numbers are 11, 13, 17 and 19. Each has only 1 and itself as positive factors.
List all the positive factors of 24. || $1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24$ || Find pairs whose product is 24. || The factor pairs are $1\times24$, $2\times12$, $3\times8$ and $4\times6$.
What is the least positive number divisible by both 6 and 8? || $24$ || Compare the multiples of 6 with the multiples of 8. || The first common positive multiple is $24=4\times6=3\times8$.
There are 36 red counters and 48 green counters. They are put into as many identical bags as possible, with no counters left over. How many bags are made? || $12$ || The number of bags must divide both quantities. || The greatest common factor of 36 and 48 is 12. Each bag then contains 3 red and 4 green counters.
Which digit makes $4\square2$ divisible by 9? || $3$ || Add the three digits. || The digit sum is $6+\square$. The only multiple of 9 from 6 to 15 is 9, so the missing digit is 3.
Write 72 as a product of prime powers. || $2^{3}\times3^{2}$ || Repeatedly divide by the smallest prime factor. || $72=2\times36=2\times2\times18=2\times2\times2\times9=2^{3}\times3^{2}$.
How many positive factors does 36 have? || $9$ || List them in pairs, counting the square root only once. || The factors are $1,2,3,4,6,9,12,18,36$, for a total of 9.
One bell rings every 8 minutes and another every 12 minutes. Both ring at 9:00. At what time will they next ring together? || 9:24 || Find their least common multiple. || The least common multiple of 8 and 12 is 24, so they next ring together 24 minutes after 9:00.
What is the smallest whole number greater than 50 that is divisible by both 6 and 9? || $54$ || Such a number must be a multiple of 18. || The common multiples are $18,36,54,72,\ldots$. The first greater than 50 is 54.
Which digits can replace the square so that $5\square4$ is divisible by both 3 and 4? || $0\text{ or }6$ || Apply the digit-sum test and the last-two-digits test. || Divisibility by 3 allows digits $0,3,6,9$. Divisibility by 4 requires an even tens digit here. Their intersection is $0,6$.
How many integers from 1 to 100 are divisible by exactly one of 2 and 5? || $50$ || Numbers divisible by both must be excluded from each count. || There are 50 multiples of 2, 20 of 5 and 10 of both. Exactly one gives $(50-10)+(20-10)=50$.
A rectangle has area $60\text{ cm}^{2}$ and positive whole-number side lengths. What is its smallest possible perimeter? || $32\text{ cm}$ || Compare the factor pairs of 60. || The pairs are $(1,60),(2,30),(3,20),(4,15),(5,12),(6,10)$. The pair $(6,10)$ has the smallest sum, giving perimeter $2(6+10)=32$ cm.

!An equation says two quantities are equal. Undo operations in reverse order, doing the same thing to both sides.
!Drawing, trying small cases and comparing two equations can reveal an unknown without a long calculation.
A sequence starts at 4 and increases by 3 each time: $4,7,10,13,\ldots$. What is the next term? || $16$ || Add the common increase to 13. || $13+3=16$.

A machine doubles its input, then subtracts 3. Its output is 17. What was the input? || $10$ || Undo the subtraction before undoing the doubling. || Before subtracting 3, the value was 20. Half of 20 is 10.

Two consecutive whole numbers have a sum of 51. What is the larger number? || $26$ || They differ by 1. || Removing the extra 1 from the sum leaves 50, split into two groups of 25. The numbers are 25 and 26.

Stage 1 of a pattern has 5 tiles. Each new stage adds 4 tiles. Which stage has 53 tiles? || $13$ || Count the additions after stage 1. || There are $(53-5)\div4=12$ additions. They lead from stage 1 to stage 13.
A two-digit positive integer is three times the sum of its digits. What is the integer? || $27$ || Let its tens digit be $a$ and its units digit be $b$. || $10a+b=3(a+b)$ gives $7a=2b$. Since $1\leq a\leq9$ and $0\leq b\leq9$, the only possibility is $a=2,b=7$. The integer is 27.
